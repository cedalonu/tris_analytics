# Evolution, game modalities, and multi-proposal strategy — research design

## Objective
Research consistent historical/temporal tendencies rather than claim to predict a random draw exactly. Evaluate whether patterns improve prospectively and reproducibly. Maintain a diverse portfolio of proposals first; reduce proposal count only when evidence and coverage/utility trade-offs support it.

## Temporal evolution (lags)
Analyze prior draws at lag -1, -2, -3 and other windows justified by discovery. Features include same-position repeat rates, position-to-position digit movement, whole-draw digit overlap, repeated digits, pair/triple transitions, high/low shifts, concentration/distribution, and regime changes. Aggregate across chronological target intervals to estimate transition matrices; single-draw comparisons are descriptive only. Conditional estimates must show counts and uncertainty and be compared with appropriate baselines. Avoid leakage: prediction for draw t uses only outcomes before t.

## Modalities and digit slices
Track predictions/results separately for:
- full five digits;
- last four digits (positions 2–5);
- last three (3–5);
- first two (1–2);
- last two (4–5);
- first and last digit (positions 1 and 5).
The exact award/prize schedule and rules must be sourced and verified before calculating expected value or optimizing portfolio size. This is research-only; do not assume prizes, costs, or odds.

## Proposal portfolio, not one prediction
Generate several candidate proposals per modality, using distinct methods and diversity constraints. Measure:
- coverage and overlap between proposals;
- number of distinct digit patterns represented;
- performance/hit distribution by modality and by prize category only where official rules confirm definitions;
- proposal count versus coverage/expense trade-off;
- diversity/duplication and concentration.
Use historical and prospective tests to compare portfolio sizes and gradually reduce the number of proposals only if coverage/utility remains robust. Preserve versioned portfolio decisions and all rejected alternatives. No proposal is guaranteed.

## Research lifecycle
1. Explore trends on discovery data.
2. Freeze hypotheses, methods and proposal generation.
3. Validate on later chronological segment.
4. Holdout test once for frozen selection, if available and not previously used.
5. Prospective evaluation on new draws.
6. Review evidence, stability, data quality, and portfolio trade-offs; adjust only with versioned notes.

## Integrity
No invented outcomes, prize schedules, or costs. Distinguish descriptive trends from statistically validated effects. A lottery may be random; historical patterns can be informative descriptions without predictive advantage.
